# Field notes — Marta Bianchi interview

**These are the designer's notes, not evidence.** What I observed beyond the words: where she looked, what she did with the phone, what she skipped, how the tone changed. If a finding from the evidence note is questioned later, the check is the transcript — not these notes, and not my memory of the session.

- Arrived from Monza on the 07:4x, coffee already in hand, sat down already talking — the commute clearly didn't cost her energy today, only attention. Kept her back to the concourse; chose the seat facing me, away from the boards.
- Phone face-down on the table for most of the interview. Exceptions, in order: (1) at "which app?" she picked it up unprompted and showed me the map-based app — scrolled the live train map, found "her" train instantly, clearly rehearsed; (2) did **not** open the official app when I asked — "no point, it updates late" — she described it instead; (3) the buzz at [00:14:52] was a Slack ping, glanced and ignored, and the timing is interesting: it landed exactly on "do you plan the night before?" — work already pressing on the same minute the commute question does.
- Twice while saying "app first, board second" she looked over her shoulder toward the concourse departure board — the board is physically present in her mental model even two floors from her platform. Gestured with two hands at "triangulation" — she knows the word, resents the activity.
- Tone map: light and fast on routine; faster and more animated on the workaround (the triangulation, the two-app check) — she's proud of the system, that's the part she *owns*. Marked drop at February: slower, longer pauses, eyes went to the table. "The man next to me called his office on speaker" was the line she repeated later in the conversation, unprompted — I think that image stayed with her.
- "I'd rather be late" [00:06:15] said with a laugh but she shook her head right after — laughed at the memory, not at the choice. Running in heels was a dignity story, not a time story.
- Skipped / deflected: the taxi part of the alternatives question (waved it off — "taxi from Monza is not a thing"); the "what did you do" question after February got a literal "Nothing. What can you do." — the shortest answer of the interview, and the most resigned.
- She checked the time twice in the last five minutes — 08:20 was her own hard stop, the buffer is real and it's hers, not the train's.
- My own note to check later: she says the official app "updates late", yet it's still her first check every morning — first ≠ trusted. The map app is trusted, the official one is merely first. Don't smooth this into "she distrusts the official app" in the evidence note; the transcript's words are more precise than my summary.
- Interview technique note: I pushed "is it reliable?" early and she laughed before answering — the laugh carried the answer. The follow-ups wrote themselves after that; guide held, no forcing needed.
