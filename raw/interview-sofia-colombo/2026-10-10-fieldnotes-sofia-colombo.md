# Field notes — Sofia Colombo interview

**These are the designer's notes, not evidence.** What I observed beyond the words: where she looked, what she did with the phone, what she skipped, how the tone changed. If a finding from the evidence note is questioned later, the check is the transcript — not these notes, and not my memory of the session.

- Interview scheduled after her Saturday shift, 16:30 — she arrived early, was already seated, phone on the table face-up. Of the three, the only one who put the phone face-up on the table at the start. The plan is a document she lives in, not a tool she picks up.
- When I asked about the night-before routine she showed me *the folder*, unprompted: dozens of train screenshots, organized by date, weeks of them. This is an archive, not a habit. Marta demonstrated a live app; Sofia showed me a filing system. The screenshot is her interface — more than the app itself.
- The app itself she opened once, scrolled briefly, closed. The screenshot got the tour.
- Tone: fast, organized, speaks in plans and numbers ("twenty is the number where I stop doing math in my head"). She enjoyed describing her own systems — proud, like Marta with the triangulation, but domesticated: no resentment, just maintenance.
- The Sunday story: everything slowed. At "choosing every word" [00:06:58] she stopped gesturing entirely — hands in her lap. The shame is social, not temporal: she said "the fifteen minutes is nothing. Everyone seeing you arrive late is the thing" — but note for later: she described the shutters and the alarm in detail. The image is visual, specific, rehearsed. This is the story she tells.
- Immediately after the insult of the morning, she corrected herself toward fairness: "Sorry. That's unfair. It's usually good at the truth. It was wrong once." She polices her own exaggeration in real time — but the loyalty is repetition-based ("right the last two hundred times"), and repetition-based loyalty breaks on the first exception. Her words, not my inference — the inference goes in Signals only.
- Word of mouth, again, unprompted: the dog man at Lodi, the platform as an announcement system — same network Ferri described at Bergamo, different station, different generation. Two independent accounts of the same layer. Worth a connections pass later.
- Skipped: the crowding question — she looked genuinely puzzled, "I sit every time." Off-peak crowding is not her problem; I did not push it into the evidence note. Also gently declined the alternatives question with arithmetic, not emotion: bus at 10-something, taxi half a day's pay. Her constraints are budget-shaped.
- Her mother calls during train rides — the commute is socially scheduled around the shift plan. Note the pattern: her whole commute is relational (shifts → manager → mom → regulars at the station).
- She ended with a brief for us: "fix the Sunday thing." She framed the problem as an app bug with a social cost, not as her failure. Two years of punctuality, one Sunday, and the system — not the commuter — got the blame. That framing is the interview's real gift.
