[00:01:31] Interviewer: How often do you do it?

[00:02:05] Interviewer: When do you figure out which train?

[00:02:11] Sofia: The night before, always. I sit down when I get home, open the app, find the train that gets me there twenty minutes early — not ten, twenty — and then I screenshot it. The time, the train number, the platform if it's already there, and the two after it, in case. The app doesn't love being reopened in a hurry, and I don't love standing on the platform reopening it. So — a picture. The picture doesn't crash.

[00:02:58] Interviewer: You said twenty minutes early — why twenty?

[00:03:04] Sofia: Because ten is hope. Twenty covers — the train being late, the metro, the line at the shop door before opening. Twenty is the number where I stop doing math in my head. My colleagues think I'm crazy. But they're the ones texting "I'm on the escalator" at 08:58.

[00:03:47] Interviewer: What's the off-peak commute like, compared to rush hour?

[00:03:55] Sofia: Empty, mostly. I sit every time, even mid-morning. Sunday morning it's quiet, families going the other way, to Lodi, for the river, I think. But the thing about off-peak: it's quiet and it's thin. In the morning peak there's another train six minutes behind yours. Off-peak, Sunday off-peak, there's nothing behind you. One every half hour, and some that just don't run on Sunday. Off-peak forgives nothing.

[00:04:51] Interviewer: Tell me about the last time something went wrong.

[00:05:02] Sofia: [pause] A Sunday. Three weeks ago. My shift was 09:30, so I took the — the 08:52, gets me to Centrale 09:22, shop with the walk, I had it planned to the minute. I get to Lodi at 08:40, I look at the board, and the 08:52 is not on it. Not delayed. Not there. Canceled doesn't even show. And the app still says it's running — I'm refreshing, because I don't believe the board, I believe the app, because the app was right the last two hundred times.

[00:06:04] Sofia: So I go to the info point, and the guy — very kind, actually — he says: "It was canceled this morning, the app doesn't always update on Sundays." The app doesn't always update on Sundays. That's — that's the sentence. That's the whole thing. The next one was 09:32. Forty minutes on the platform.

[00:06:53] Interviewer: What did you do?

[00:06:58] Sofia: I called my manager from the platform. First time in two years. I was — you know when your voice is fine but you're choosing every word? She said it's fine, and it was fine. But I walked in fifteen minutes late, the alarm was already off, the shutters half up. Everyone saw me arrive. That's the part. The fifteen minutes is nothing. Everyone seeing you arrive late is the thing.

[00:07:51] Interviewer: Did the app tell you anything at all, after?

[00:07:56] Sofia: Nothing. No notification, nothing. It still had the 08:52 as "on time" — I looked later, when I was already at the shop. It corrected at like 09:10. After the train didn't exist. So the app was right, eventually, about a train that was never going to come.
