# Field notes — Alessandro Ferri interview

**These are the designer's notes, not evidence.** What I observed beyond the words: where he looked, what he did with the phone, what he skipped, how the tone changed. If a finding from the evidence note is questioned later, the check is the transcript — not these notes, and not my memory of the session.

- Older phone, cracked corner, kept in his jacket most of the time. He never showed me an app — the opposite of Marta's demo. When he said "the app" he gestured vaguely at his pocket; the *board* he pointed at with his chin toward the concourse.
- Exactly one board-glance, mid-sentence, while saying "always the board first" — a reflex, done while talking, not performed for me. The board is muscle memory; the app is a tool he resents.
- The crowding system is spatial for him: he laid out Bergamo platform on the café table with two cups and a sugar packet — stairs, herd cluster, front car, "and I'm here" (the sugar at the far end). He has drawn this map before; the coffee was rehearsed.
- Tone: short laughs all the way through — fatalist's laugh, punctuating, not amused. Everything is "an honest price", "my own punishment" — he narrates the commute as a deal he accepted. The laughter stopped completely only once: the girl apologizing to her boss. That story came quieter, and he looked past me at the concourse while telling it.
- Interesting inversion at [00:04:52]: "the days I sit, I like the ride" — said as a confession, slightly embarrassed ("There. I said it."). The affection for the commute only exists when it's seated and predictable.
- Internal inconsistency, left as-is in the transcript: "fifty minutes" at [00:00:44], self-corrected immediately to "just under an hour door to door". Don't smooth this into one number in the evidence note — his lived number is soft, Marta's is precise. That difference is itself a finding about how each of them relates to the timetable.
- Second thing to check, not resolve: he says he never plans the night before [00:13:44], then describes charging devices and downloading podcasts the night before — "loading the bag". He doesn't count preparation as planning, because planning implies *options* to him. His words; keep the distinction.
- Deflections: the alternatives question got a hand-wave before I finished it ("there is no other way"); the "what did you do during the delay" question got a second, more honest pass from him on his own ("I was fine, and I watched other people not be fine") — he self-corrects toward honesty when given silence. Lesson for me: don't rush the follow-ups.
- He asked me at the end whether other commuters say the same about the delay numbers — wanted to compare notes. Wants validation that his discount rule is universal. The topic has social currency.
- Time discipline: he did not check the clock once. Marta watched the clock; Ferri watches the *board*. Two different relationships to the same twenty-five minutes.
