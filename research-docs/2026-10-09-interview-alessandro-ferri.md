# Interview — Alessandro Ferri, long-ride regional commuter

Interviewed at Milano Centrale after his morning arrival, twenty-five minutes, recorded with consent. Verbatim transcript: `raw/2026-10-09-transcript-alessandro-ferri.md`; designer's observations from the session: `raw/2026-10-09-fieldnotes-alessandro-ferri.md`. Quotes below reference the transcript timestamps. Confidence is **medium**: his time figures are soft (fifty minutes / "just under an hour" [00:00:44]) and his claims about the app's behavior are his own rule of thumb, not a measurement.

## Who he is

Forty-one, consultant, lives in Bergamo since a move two years ago ("the price of the house with the garden"), in a Milan office Tuesday to Thursday. The 07:05 regional via Treviglio, roughly fifty minutes on the rail, a "just under an hour" door to door [00:00:44].

## Crowding decides the quality of the ride

The ride is binary for him: seated it is thinking time, standing it is endurance. His whole strategy exists to guarantee the seated version — front car, first doors, because "nobody walks to the very front — they come up the stairs and they stop, herd behavior" [00:02:51]. The strategy fails when the train arrives short:

> "Standing, fifty minutes is just endurance. You do email one-handed. There's no thinking." — [00:03:47]

## The waiting is planned, and it is not dead

He prepares content the night before — but does not call it planning, because planning "is for people with options" [00:13:44]. Preparation is "loading the bag": podcasts and news downloaded at home, because "after Treviglio the signal is theoretical" [00:04:52]. The morning wait at Bergamo is nothing; the evening wait at Centrale before the 18:10 is the one wait he values:

> "It's decompression. Work is still in my head on the platform; by Bergamo it's gone." — [00:06:05]

## The board first, then the app he does not trust, then the platform

His information chain is the reverse of Marta's: board first (he is standing in front of it anyway), app after — and the app is not consulted but discounted:

> "The app says five minutes late, the board says five minutes late, and the train arrives fifteen minutes late. They tell you the number they hope. Every time." — [00:07:24]

What he actually trusts is the platform itself:

> "The platform is the real app. Someone always asks him, and the answer travels down the platform like — you can watch it move. That's the announcement system." — [00:08:10]

## The same delay is not the same delay for everyone

The last bad morning: held twenty-five to thirty minutes outside Treviglio, one announcement in Italian only, then silence. His own cost was zero — no morning meeting, podcast already playing. But he watched a fellow passenger apologize to her boss twice by phone during the hold [00:09:02]. His summary of the episode, unprompted:

> "I was fine, and I watched other people not be fine." — [00:10:38]

## What he wants is the morning before the morning

Not a better notification at the platform — information *before the decision to leave home*:

> "If the line is sick in the morning, tell me at 06:30, and it's a choice — take the 07:05 anyway, or the 07:35, or work from home and take the 09:15 empty. Instead you find out at Treviglio, standing, when the choice is already gone. The information exists. It's just — later than the decision." — [00:11:44]

And when a delay must be told, the honest number over the optimistic one:

> "Say twenty, and twenty comes, I'm calm. Say five and deliver fifteen, and you've made me a cynic." — [00:12:47]

## Signals — reading, not evidence

Mapping to the brief's pain points:

- **Overcrowding and discomfort** — the strongest signal in this interview: the commute's quality is decided by seated vs standing, and he runs a whole spatial strategy to control it.
- **Uncertainty about arrival times** — the delay number is systematically discounted in his experience ("the number they hope"). This contradicts Marta's picture of the map app as the trustworthy channel — see the contradictions check in the index review. It is also primary evidence for the "drifting delay estimates" item left pending in `research-docs/2026-10-05-desk-delay-information.md`.
- **Boredom during waiting** — inverted: waiting can be valued (evening decompression); the dead time is not waiting itself but the *unplannable* time — a hold between stations with no usable information.
- **Information scattered / word of mouth** — the platform is a working information network the operator does not run: the regulars, the man who knows the drivers. Confirms the "social layer" left pending in the desk note — for his line, it is the *primary* layer.
- **Recovering from a delay** — his account of the same hold costing him nothing and the girl her standing at work: the cost of a delay is a function of what is waiting at the other end, not of the minutes.

Strongest candidate moment from this interview: **the morning before the morning** — honest line-level information delivered before the leave-home decision. Complementary, not identical, to Marta's delay explanation.
